# BIOMEDICAL IMPORTANCE

Bioenergetics is the study of the energy changes accompanying biochemical reactions. Biological systems are essentially isothermic. Biological systems use chemical energy to power living processes. An animal obtains suitable fuel from its food to provide energy. Understanding this process is basic to the understanding of normal nutrition and metabolism. Death from starvation occurs when available energy reserves are depleted. Certain forms of malnutrition are associated with energy imbalance, such as marasmus. Thyroid hormones control the metabolic rate. The metabolic rate is the rate of energy release. Disease results if thyroid hormones malfunction. Excess storage of surplus energy causes obesity. Obesity is an increasingly common disease in Western society. Obesity predisposes to many diseases, including cardiovascular disease and diabetes mellitus type 2. Obesity lowers life expectancy.

# FREE ENERGY IS THE USEFUL ENERGY IN A SYSTEM

## Biologic Systems Conform to the General Laws of Thermodynamics

The first law of thermodynamics states that the total energy of a system, including its surroundings, remains constant. The first law implies that within the total system, energy is neither lost nor gained during any change. Energy may be transferred from one part of the system to another. Energy may be transformed into another form of energy. In living systems, chemical energy may be transformed into heat, electrical, radiant, or mechanical energy.

The second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of a system must increase if a process is to occur spontaneously. Entropy is the extent of disorder or randomness of the system. Entropy becomes maximum as equilibrium is approached. T is the absolute temperature.

When the reactants are present in concentrations of 1.0 mol/L, \(\Delta G^{0}\) is the standard free-energy change. For biochemical reactions, a standard state is defined as having a pH of 7.0. The standard state is denoted by \(\Delta G^{0'}\). The standard free-energy change can be calculated from the equilibrium constant \(K_{eq}\) using the equation \(\Delta G^{0\prime} = -RT\ln K_{\text{eq}}\). In this equation, R is the gas constant. In this equation, T is the absolute temperature. The actual \(\Delta G\) may be larger or smaller than \(\Delta G^{0}\) depending on the concentrations of the various reactants, including the solvent, various ions, and proteins.

In a biochemical system, an enzyme speeds up the attainment of equilibrium. In a biochemical system, an enzyme never alters the final concentrations of the reactants at equilibrium.

# ENDERGONIC PROCESSES PROCEED BY COUPLING TO EXERGONIC PROCESSES

The vital processes obtain energy by coupling to oxidative reactions. Biosynthetic reactions are vital processes. Muscular contraction is a vital process. Nerve impulse conduction is a vital process. Active transport is a vital process. The term exergonic indicates a process that involves a loss of free energy. The term endergonic indicates a process that involves a gain of free energy. An endergonic process cannot exist independently. An endergonic process must be part of a coupled exergonic-endergonic system. In a coupled exergonic-endergonic system, the overall net change is exergonic. Exergonic reactions are termed catabolism. Catabolism involves the breakdown or oxidation of fuel molecules. Anabolic reactions build up substances. The combined catabolic and anabolic processes constitute metabolism.

# HIGH-ENERGY PHOSPHATES PLAY A CENTRAL ROLE IN ENERGY CAPTURE & TRANSFER

All organisms must obtain supplies of free energy from their environment to maintain life. Autotrophic organisms utilize simple exergonic processes to obtain free energy. Some autotrophic organisms use the energy of sunlight, like green plants. Heterotrophic organisms obtain free energy by coupling their metabolism to the breakdown of complex organic molecules in their environment. ATP plays a central role in the transference of free energy from exergonic to endergonic processes in all organisms. ATP is a nucleotide consisting of the nucleoside adenosine and three phosphate groups. Adenosine is adenine linked to ribose.

## Adenosine diphosphate (ADP) Adenosine monophosphate (AMP)

ATP plays an important role in energy transfer due to its intermediate position. The high free-energy change on hydrolysis of ATP is not caused by breaking the P-O bond linking the terminal phosphate to the molecule. Energy is needed to break the P-O bond linking the terminal phosphate to the molecule. The strong electrostatic repulsion between the negatively charged oxygen atoms in the adjacent phosphate groups of ATP destabilizes the molecule. The destabilization of ATP by electrostatic repulsion makes the removal of one phosphate group energetically favorable. The orthophosphate produced is greatly stabilized by the formation of resonance hybrids. The resonance hybrids in orthophosphate share the three negative charges between the four oxygen atoms. Overall, the products of hydrolysis, ADP and orthophosphate, are more stable and lower in energy than ATP (Figure 11–5).

This, and the stabilization of the orthophosphate released (3) more than compensate for the initial energy input (1) and result in the high free-energy change seen when ATP is hydrolyzed to ADP.

## ATP Allows the Coupling of Thermodynamically Unfavorable Reactions to Favorable Ones

Endergonic reactions cannot proceed without an input of free energy. The phosphorylation of glucose to glucose-6-phosphate is an example of an endergonic reaction. The phosphorylation of glucose to glucose-6-phosphate is the first step of glycolysis. The standard free energy change for this reaction is +13.8 kJ/mol. This positive ΔG indicates that the phosphorylation of glucose to glucose-6-phosphate is highly endergonic and cannot proceed under physiologic conditions without additional energy input. The hydrolysis of ATP is an exergonic reaction that can provide the necessary energy. The standard free energy change for ATP hydrolysis is -30.5 kJ/mol. This negative ΔG indicates that the hydrolysis of ATP is highly exergonic. When the phosphorylation of glucose and the hydrolysis of ATP are coupled, the overall reaction becomes exergonic. The coupling of these reactions is catalyzed by the enzyme hexokinase. The coupled reaction allows the phosphorylation of glucose to proceed readily and irreversibly under physiologic conditions. Many activation reactions in biochemistry follow this pattern of coupling endergonic reactions with exergonic ATP hydrolysis.
