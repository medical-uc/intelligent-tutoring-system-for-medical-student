# BIOMEDICAL IMPORTANCE

Dietary purines are not directly incorporated into tissue nucleic acids. Dietary pyrimidines are not directly incorporated into tissue nucleic acids. Humans synthesize nucleic acids from amphibolic intermediates. Humans synthesize ATP from amphibolic intermediates. Humans synthesize coenzyme A from amphibolic intermediates. Injected purine analogs may be incorporated into DNA. Injected pyrimidine analogs may be incorporated into DNA. The biosyntheses of purine ribonucleotide triphosphates are precisely regulated. The biosyntheses of pyrimidine ribonucleotide triphosphates are precisely regulated. The biosyntheses of purine dNTPs are precisely regulated. The biosyntheses of pyrimidine dNTPs are precisely regulated. Feedback mechanisms coordinate the production of purine and pyrimidine ribonucleotide triphosphates to match varying physiologic demand. Feedback mechanisms coordinate the production of purine and pyrimidine dNTPs to match varying physiologic demand. Cell division is an example of varying physiologic demand.

Human diseases involving abnormalities in purine metabolism include gout, Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, adenosine deaminase deficiency, and purine nucleoside phosphorylase deficiency. Diseases of pyrimidine biosynthesis are rarer but include orotic acidurias. Uric acid is formed by purine catabolism. Uric acid has low solubility. The end products of pyrimidine catabolism are highly water soluble.

# PURINES & PYRIMIDINES ARE DIETARILY NONESSENTIAL

Normal human tissues can synthesize purines and pyrimidines from amphibolic intermediates. Ingested nucleic acids and nucleotides are dietarily nonessential. The resulting mononucleotides from their degradation in the intestinal tract may be absorbed or converted to purine and pyrimidine bases. The purine bases are oxidized to uric acid. Uric acid may be absorbed and excreted in the urine. Little or no dietary purine or pyrimidine is incorporated into tissue nucleic acids. Injected compounds are incorporated into tissue nucleic acids. Radiolabeled uracil can be used to measure the rate of cellular RNA synthesis. Radiolabeled thymidine can be used to measure the rate of cellular DNA synthesis.

With the exception of parasitic protozoa, all forms of life synthesize purine and pyrimidine nucleotides. NTPs rise during growth or tissue regeneration when cells are rapidly dividing. Purine and pyrimidine nucleotides are synthesized in vivo at rates consistent with physiologic need. Conversion of purines, their ribonucleosides, and their deoxyribonucleosides to mononucleotides involves 'salvage reactions' that require far less energy than de novo synthesis. Phosphoribosylation by PRPP of a free purine (Pu) forms a purine 5'-mononucleotide (Pu-RP).

# THE DEOXYRIBONUCLEOSIDES OF URACIL & CYTOSINE ARE SALVAGED

Adenine, guanine, and hypoxanthine are reconverted to nucleoside triphosphates via salvage pathways. Mammalian cells reutilize few free pyrimidines. Salvage reactions convert the pyrimidine ribonucleosides uridine and cytidine and the pyrimidine deoxyribonucleosides thymidine and deoxycytidine to their respective nucleotides. Guanine and PRPP react to form GMP and PPi. Hypoxanthine and PRPP react to form IMP and PPi. The conversion of dNDPs to nucleoside triphosphates results in the corresponding nucleoside triphosphates. NDP and ATP react to form NTP and ADP. dNDP and ATP react to form dNTP and ADP.

## Methotrexate Blocks Reduction of Dihydrofolate

The reaction catalyzed by thymidylate synthase, EC 2.1.1.45, is the only reaction of pyrimidine nucleotide biosynthesis that requires a tetrahydrofolate derivative. Dihydrofolate is the oxidized form of tetrahydrofolate. For further pyrimidine synthesis to occur, dihydrofolate must be reduced back to tetrahydrofolate. The reduction of dihydrofolate is catalyzed by dihydrofolate reductase (EC 1.5.1.3). Methotrexate inhibits the reduction of dihydrofolate by dihydrofolate reductase. Dividing cells are especially sensitive to inhibitors of dihydrofolate reductase such as methotrexate. Methotrexate is an anticancer drug.

# DISORDERS OF PURINE METABOLISM

Genetic defects in PRPP synthetase can present clinically as gout. Increased affinity for ribose 5-phosphate in PRPP synthetase results in overproduction and overexcretion of purine catabolites. Resistance to feedback inhibition in PRPP synthetase causes overproduction and overexcretion of purine catabolites. When serum urate levels exceed the solubility limit, sodium urate crystallizes in soft tissues and joints. Sodium urate crystals cause an inflammatory reaction known as gouty arthritis. Most cases of gout reflect abnormalities in renal handling of uric acid.

Purine deficiency states are rare in human subjects. There are numerous genetic disorders of purine catabolism. Hyperuricemias can be differentiated based on whether patients excrete normal or excessive quantities of total urates. Some hyperuricemias reflect specific enzyme defects. Other hyperuricemias are secondary to diseases such as cancer or psoriasis that enhance tissue turnover.

## Lesch-Nyhan Syndrome

The Lesch-Nyhan syndrome is characterized by frequent episodes of uric acid lithiasis and a bizarre syndrome of self-mutilation.

The Lesch-Nyhan syndrome reflects a defect in hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyl transferase, an enzyme of purine salvage.

The defect in hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyl transferase leads to an accompanying rise in intracellular PRPP.

The rise in intracellular PRPP results in purine overproduction.

Mutations that decrease or abolish hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase activity include deletions, frame-shift mutations, base substitutions, and aberrant mRNA splicing.

## von Gierke Disease

von Gierke disease involves purine overproduction and hyperuricemia.

von Gierke disease is characterized by glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency.

Enhanced generation of the PRPP precursor ribose 5-phosphate leads to purine overproduction in von Gierke disease.

An associated lactic acidosis in von Gierke disease elevates the renal threshold for urate.

Elevated renal threshold for urate results in elevated total body urates in von Gierke disease.

## Hypouricemia

Hypouricemia is associated with a deficiency in xanthine oxidase, EC 1.17.3.2. Increased excretion of hypoxanthine and xanthine is associated with a deficiency in xanthine oxidase, EC 1.17.3.2. A deficiency in xanthine oxidase can be due to a genetic defect. A deficiency in xanthine oxidase can be due to severe liver damage. Patients with a severe enzyme deficiency may exhibit xanthinuria. Patients with a severe enzyme deficiency may exhibit xanthine lithiasis.

## Adenosine Deaminase & Purine Nucleoside Phosphorylase Deficiency

Adenosine deaminase deficiency is associated with an immunodeficiency disease.

In this disease, thymus-derived lymphocytes (T cells) are sparse and dysfunctional.

In this disease, bone marrow-derived lymphocytes (B cells) are sparse and dysfunctional.

Patients with adenosine deaminase deficiency suffer from severe immunodeficiency.

Without enzyme replacement or bone marrow transplantation, infants with adenosine deaminase deficiency often succumb to fatal infections.

Defective activity of purine nucleoside phosphorylase (EC 2.4.2.1) is associated with a severe deficiency of T cells.

In the case of purine nucleoside phosphorylase deficiency, B-cell function appears normal.

Immune dysfunctions in these deficiencies result from the accumulation of dGTP and dATP.

dGTP and dATP inhibit ribonucleotide reductase.

The inhibition of ribonucleotide reductase depletes cells of DNA precursors.

# OVERPRODUCTION OF PYRIMIDINE CATABOLITES

Since the end products of pyrimidine catabolism are highly water soluble, pyrimidine overproduction results in few clinical signs or symptoms.

## Orotic Aciduria

Orotic aciduria accompanies the Reye syndrome due to the inability of severely damaged mitochondria to utilize carbamoyl phosphate.

Carbamoyl phosphate becomes available for cytosolic overproduction of orotic acid when mitochondria are severely damaged.

Type I orotic aciduria reflects a deficiency of both orotate phosphoribosyltransferase and orotidylate decarboxylase.

Type II orotic aciduria is due to a deficiency only of orotidylate decarboxylase.

Type II orotic aciduria is rarer than Type I orotic aciduria.

## Deficiency of a Urea Cycle Enzyme Results in Excretion of Pyrimidine Precursors

A deficiency in liver mitochondrial ornithine transcarbamoylase leads to increased excretion of orotic acid, uracil, and uridine.

Excess carbamoyl phosphate exits to the cytosol when liver mitochondrial ornithine transcarbamoylase is deficient.

In the cytosol, excess carbamoyl phosphate stimulates pyrimidine nucleotide biosynthesis.

The deficiency in liver mitochondrial ornithine transcarbamoylase results in mild orotic aciduria.

High-nitrogen foods increase the mild orotic aciduria associated with the deficiency in liver mitochondrial ornithine transcarbamoylase.

## Drugs May Precipitate Orotic Aciduria

Allopurinol is an alternative substrate for orotate phosphoribosyltransferase. Allopurinol competes with orotic acid. The nucleotide product resulting from allopurinol inhibits orotidylate decarboxylase. Inhibition of orotidylate decarboxylase by the nucleotide product results in orotic aciduria. Inhibition of orotidylate decarboxylase by the nucleotide product results in orotidinuria.

6-Azauridine converts to 6-azauridylate. 6-Azauridylate competitively inhibits orotidylate decarboxylase. Inhibition of orotidylate decarboxylase by 6-azauridylate enhances excretion of orotic acid. Inhibition of orotidylate decarboxylase by 6-azauridylate enhances excretion of orotidine.

Four genes encode urate transporters. Two of these encoded proteins are localized to the apical membrane of proximal tubular cells.

Degradation of ingested nucleic acids yields free purines and pyrimidines. Purines and pyrimidines are formed from amphibolic intermediates and thus are dietarily nonessential.
Several reactions of IMP biosynthesis require folate derivatives and glutamine. Consequently, antifolate drugs and glutamine analogs inhibit purine biosynthesis.
IMP is a precursor both of AMP and GMP. Glutamine provides the 2-amino group of GMP, and aspartate the 6-amino group of AMP.
Phosphoryl transfer from ATP converts AMP and GMP to ADP and GDP. A second phosphoryl transfer from ATP forms GTP, but ADP is converted to ATP primarily by oxidative phosphorylation.
Hepatic purine nucleotide biosynthesis is stringently regulated by the pool size of PRPP and by feedback inhibition of PRPP glutamyl amidotransferase by AMP and GMP.
Coordinated regulation of purine and pyrimidine nucleotide biosynthesis ensures their presence in proportions appropriate for nucleic acid biosynthesis and other metabolic needs.
Urate crystals are diagnostic of gout. Other disorders of purine catabolism include Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, von Gierke disease, and hypouricemias.
Since pyrimidine catabolites are water soluble, their overproduction does not result in clinical abnormalities. Excretion of pyrimidine precursors can, however, result from a deficiency of ornithine transcarbamoylase because excess carbamoyl phosphate is available for pyrimidine biosynthesis.
