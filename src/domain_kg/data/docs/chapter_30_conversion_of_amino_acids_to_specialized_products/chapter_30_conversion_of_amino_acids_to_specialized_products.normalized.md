# BIOMEDICAL IMPORTANCE

Certain proteins contain amino acids that have been posttranslationally modified to perform specialized functions. Proline is hydroxylated to form 3- and 4-hydroxyproline in collagen. Lysine is hydroxylated to 5-hydroxylysine, which stabilizes maturing collagen fibers through modification and cross-linking.

Amino acids serve as precursors for diverse biologic materials including heme, purines, pyrimidines, hormones, neurotransmitters, and biologically active peptides. Histamine plays a central role in many allergic reactions. Many drugs used to treat neurologic and psychiatric conditions alter the metabolism of these neurotransmitters.

## Alanine

Alanine serves as a carrier of ammonia and of the carbons of pyruvate from skeletal muscle to the liver via the Cori cycle.

Alanine, together with glycine, constitutes a major fraction of the free amino acids in plasma.

## Arginine

Arginine serves as a carrier of nitrogen atoms in urea biosynthesis. The guanidino group of arginine is incorporated into creatine. Arginine is converted to ornithine, and its carbon skeleton serves as a precursor of the polyamines putrescine and spermine. Putrescine and spermine synthesis occurs in both mammals and bacteria (Figure 30-1).

## Glycine

Glycine conjugation converts many relatively apolar metabolites into water-soluble compounds. Hippuric acid is an example of a glycine conjugate formed from benzoate, a food additive (Figure 30--4). Many drugs, drug metabolites, and other compounds with carboxyl groups are conjugated with glycine. Glycine conjugation increases their water solubility. Increased water solubility facilitates the excretion of these compounds in the urine. Similar reactions occur with many acidic drugs and catabolites (Figure 30--4).

## Methionine

The major nonprotein fate of methionine is its conversion to S-adenosylmethionine, which is the principal source of methyl groups in the body. S-adenosylmethionine is synthesized from methionine and ATP by the enzyme methionine adenosyltransferase (MAT), classified as EC 2.5.1.6. Human tissues contain three isozymes of MAT: MAT-1 and MAT-3 are found in the liver, while MAT-2 is present in nonhepatic tissues. If hepatic MAT-1 and MAT-3 activity is severely decreased, hypermethioninemia can result. However, if there is residual MAT-1 or MAT-3 activity and MAT-2 activity is normal, a high tissue concentration of methionine will ensure adequate synthesis of S-adenosylmethionine.

Polyamines are crucial for cell proliferation and growth and act as growth factors for cultured mammalian cells. Polyamines also stabilize intact cells, subcellular organelles, and membranes. Pharmacologically, polyamines can induce hypothermia and hypotension. Due to their multiple positive charges, polyamines readily associate with DNA and RNA. ## Tryptophan

Tryptophan is hydroxylated to 5-hydroxytryptophan in the liver by tryptophan hydroxylase (EC 1.14.16.4). 5-hydroxytryptophan undergoes subsequent decarboxylation to form serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine). Serotonin is a potent vasoconstrictor. Serotonin is a stimulator of smooth muscle contraction. Catabolism of serotonin is initiated by deamination to 5-hydroxyindole-3-acetate. Monoamine oxidase catalyzes the reaction of deamination of serotonin to 5-hydroxyindole-3-acetate, EC 1.4.3.4 (Figure 30–9).

[FIGURE:fig-30-9]

Biosynthesis and metabolism of serotonin and melatonin. ( [NH4+] , by transamination; MAO, monoamine oxidase; CH3 , from S-adenosylmethionine.)

Iproniazid prolongs the action of serotonin by inhibiting monoamine oxidase. Iproniazid results in psychic stimulation by inhibiting monoamine oxidase. In carcinoid tumors, tumor cells overproduce serotonin. Urinary metabolites of serotonin in patients with carcinoid include N-acetylserotonin glucuronide. Urinary metabolites of serotonin in patients with carcinoid include the glycine conjugate of 5-hydroxyindoleacetate. Serotonin is metabolized to the corresponding acids by monoamine oxidase. 5-methoxytryptamine is metabolized to the corresponding acids by monoamine oxidase.

N-Acetylation of serotonin forms melatonin in the pineal body. O-methylation follows the N-acetylation of serotonin in the pineal body. Circulating melatonin is taken up by all tissues, including the brain. Melatonin is rapidly metabolized by hydroxylation. Melatonin is conjugated with sulfate or with glucuronic acid after hydroxylation. Kidney tissue converts tryptophan to tryptamine, then to indole 3-acetate. Liver tissue converts tryptophan to tryptamine, then to indole 3-acetate. Fecal bacteria convert tryptophan to tryptamine, then to indole 3-acetate. The principal normal urinary catabolites of tryptophan are 5-hydroxyindoleacetate and indole 3-acetate (see Figure 30–9).

## Tyrosine

Neural cells convert tyrosine to epinephrine and norepinephrine (Figure 30--10). DOPA decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.28), a pyridoxal phosphate-dependent enzyme, forms dopamine from dopa. Dopamine β-oxidase (EC 1.14.17.1) catalyzes the hydroxylation of dopamine to form norepinephrine. Phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (EC 2.1.1.28) utilizes S-adenosylmethionine to methylate the primary amine of norepinephrine, forming epinephrine in the adrenal medulla. Tyrosine is a precursor of triiodothyronine and thyroxine.

Different enzymes hydroxylate tyrosine in melanocytes for the formation of melanin. Tyrosine serves as an intermediate in the formation of melanin.

## Sarcosine (N-Methylglycine)

The biosynthesis and catabolism of sarcosine (N-methylglycine) occur in mitochondria. Formation of sarcosine from dimethyl glycine is catalyzed by the flavoprotein dimethyl glycine dehydrogenase EC 1.5.8.4. The reaction requires reduced pteroylpentaglutamate (TPG).

Dimethylglycine + FADH₂ + H₄TPG + H₂O → Sarcosine + N-formyl-TPG. Traces of sarcosine can also arise by methylation of glycine. The reaction is catalyzed by glycine N-methyltransferase, EC 2.1.1.20.

Glycine + S-Adenosylmethionine → Sarcosine + S-Adenosylhomocysteine. Catabolism of sarcosine to glycine is catalyzed by the flavoprotein sarcosine dehydrogenase EC 1.5.8.3. Catabolism of sarcosine to glycine also requires reduced TPG.

The demethylation reactions that form and degrade sarcosine represent important sources of one-carbon units.

# NON-α-AMINO ACIDS

## β-Alanine & β-Aminoisobutyrate

β-Alanine is formed during the catabolism of the pyrimidine uracil. β-Aminoisobutyrate is formed during the catabolism of the pyrimidine thymine. Traces of β-alanine also result from the hydrolysis of β-alanyl dipeptides by the enzyme carnosinase, EC 3.4.13.20. β-Aminoisobutyrate also arises by transamination of methylmalonate semialdehyde, a catabolite of L-valine.

The initial reaction of β-alanine catabolism is transamination to malonate semialdehyde. Subsequent transfer of coenzyme A from succinyl-CoA forms malonyl-CoA semialdehyde. Malonyl-CoA semialdehyde is then oxidized to malonyl-CoA. Malonyl-CoA is decarboxylated to the amphibolic intermediate acetyl-CoA. Analogous reactions characterize the catabolism of β-aminoisobutyrate. Transamination forms methylmalonate semialdehyde. Methylmalonate semialdehyde is converted to the amphibolic intermediate succinyl-CoA by reactions 8V and 9V of Figure 29–22.

Disorders of β-alanine and β-aminoisobutyrate metabolism arise from defects in enzymes of the pyrimidine catabolic pathway. Principal among these are disorders that result from a total or partial deficiency of dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase.

## β-Alanyl Dipeptides

Carnosine and anserine activate myosin ATPase (EC 3.6.4.1). Carnosine and anserine chelate copper. Carnosine and anserine enhance copper uptake. Carnosine synthetase catalyzes the biosynthesis of carnosine (EC 6.3.2.11). Carnosinase deficiency is a heritable disorder characterized by carnosinuria. Homocarnosine is present in the human brain at higher levels than carnosine. Carnosine synthetase synthesizes homocarnosine. Serum carnosinase does not hydrolyze homocarnosine. Homocarnosinosis is a rare genetic disorder associated with progressive spastic paraplegia and mental retardation.

A rare genetic disorder of GABA metabolism involves a defective GABA aminotransferase EC 2.6.1.19. GABA aminotransferase participates in the catabolism of GABA subsequent to its postsynaptic release in brain tissue.

Defects in succinic semialdehyde dehydrogenase, EC 1.2.1.24 (see Figure 30-12) are responsible for 4-hydroxybutyric aciduria. 4-hydroxybutyric aciduria is a rare metabolic disorder of GABA catabolism characterized by the presence of 4-hydroxybutyrate in urine, plasma, and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).

No present treatment is available for the accompanying mild to severe neurologic symptoms of 4-hydroxybutyric aciduria.

Arginine provides the formamidine group of creatine and the nitrogen of NO. Via ornithine, arginine provides the skeleton of the polyamines putrescine, spermine, and spermidine.
Cysteine provides the thioethanolamine portion of coenzyme A and, following its conversion to taurine, is part of the bile acid taurocholic acid.
Glycine participates in the biosynthesis of heme, purines, creatine, and N-methylglycine (sarcosine). Many drugs and drug metabolites are excreted as glycine conjugates. This enhances their water solubility for urinary excretion.
Decarboxylation of histidine forms the neurotransmitter histamine. Histidine compounds present in the human body include ergothioneine, carnosine, and anserine.
S-Adenosylmethionine, the principal source of methyl groups in metabolism, contributes its carbon skeleton to the biosynthesis of the polyamines spermine and spermidine.
In addition to its roles in phospholipid and sphingosine biosynthesis, serine provides carbons 2 and 8 of purines and the methyl group of thymine.
Key tryptophan metabolites include serotonin and melatonin. Kidney and liver tissue, and also fecal bacteria, convert tryptophan to tryptamine and thence to indole 3-acetate. The principal tryptophan catabolites in urine are indole 3-acetate and 5-hydroxyindoleacetate.
Tyrosine forms norepinephrine and epinephrine, and following iodination, the thyroid hormones triiodothyronine and thyroxine.
Glycine, arginine, and S-adenosylmethionine all participate in the biosynthesis of creatine, which as creatine phosphate serves as a major energy reserve in muscle and brain tissue. Excretion in the urine of its catabolite creatinine is proportionate to muscle mass.
Decarboxylation of glutamate forms the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA. Two rare metabolic disorders are associated with defects in GABA catabolism.
